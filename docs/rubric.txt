2. THE 3 PATTERNS IN THE HIGH-RATED HIRES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PATTERN A - THEY HAVE SAT IN THE OPERATOR'S CHAIR
All 5 Exceeds hires personally carried day-to-day accountability for live freight or
logistics operations - real shipments, real documents, real counterparties - BEFORE (or
alongside) their "real" function. None of the 3 others did.

  Rohan    - Operations Executive at a CHA firm, JNPT: 180+ shipments/month, Bills of
             Lading, Shipping Bills, customs holds, DGFT licences.
  Sunita   - Documentation & Compliance Exec at a freight forwarder: 200+ shipments/month,
             CHA/port/DGFT coordination, customs inspections closed without penalty.
  Aditya   - Sales Exec at a JNPT terminal: berth allocation escalations, container
             detention negotiations, DO releases; "worked alongside the terminal
             operations team during peak periods".
  Meghna   - Customer Relations & Documentation Exec at a freight forwarder: 15-20 live
             shipments/day, LC document sets, a 7pm customs hold resolved overnight.
  Lavanya  - Supply Chain Analyst at Mahindra Logistics: carrier allocation and exception
             management for 800+ shipments/month.

  Vikram   - HR-tech SaaS only. Knows users through interviews, not through doing the job.
  Preetham - Integrated 3PL APIs (Delhivery, Bluedart) from a 12-engineer desk at a
             3,200-person e-commerce company. Touched logistics data, never logistics work.
  Rahul    - Fintech and HR-tech marketing. No operations exposure.

Why this is not in the JD: the PM JD asks for "curiosity" about ground-level operations and
wants the hire to spend time in customer rooms AFTER joining (a 6-month outcome). The SPM JD
calls domain "familiarity" an advantage. Neither asks for what actually separated the
hires: having been the person who gets the phone call when a shipment is stuck. Curiosity
is an attitude you can fake in an interview; having owned the consequences shows up as
specific documents, volumes and counterparties on a CV.

PATTERN B - THEY BUILT THE FIX NOBODY ASKED FOR, AND OTHER PEOPLE ADOPTED IT
4-5 of the 5 Exceeds hires noticed a broken workflow outside their assigned remit, built a
scrappy fix with whatever was at hand (Excel, a weekend prototype, an SOP, a checklist),
and it spread to peers or other teams without a mandate - and stayed.

  Rohan    - Excel shipment tracker (nobody knew where shipments stood) -> adopted by the
             12-person ops team in 2 weeks. BoL verification prototype built over a
             weekend -> 30 colleagues using it in month 1 -> now a core platform feature.
  Sunita   - FMS vendor changed its export format without notice -> redesigned the
             intake workflow over a weekend -> "retained permanently by the team".
  Lavanya  - Daily visibility dashboard in Excel -> migrated to Tableau "after the team
             outgrew it" -> adopted by 2 other regional teams. Built the branch's first
             carrier scorecard. Created a same-day exception triage "that hadn't existed".
  Meghna   - Onboarding checklist -> team standard (-35% onboarding queries). 30-60-90
             framework -> used by the full CS team.
  Aditya   - A salesperson who built the company's first case-study programme (normally a
             marketing job) -> now primary sales collateral. Loss post-mortem -> now
             standard qualification practice for the whole team.

  Comparison group: their artefacts sit inside their own job description. Vikram's PRD
  template is a PM producing a PM artefact for a PM team. Rahul's case studies are a
  marketer doing marketing. Preetham's monitoring dashboard served his own service.
  Nothing spread sideways into someone else's workflow.

Why this is not in the JD: the JD asks for people who have "shipped" things. Shipping on an
assigned roadmap is not the signal. The signal is (a) self-initiated, (b) outside own
remit, (c) adopted by others voluntarily, (d) still in use. This is also the best
predictor of the JD's own 6-month goal ("features customers use without being asked to"),
which the JD never translates into a hiring criterion.

PATTERN C - THEY ABSORB THE BREAK THEMSELVES - NO EXTRA HEADCOUNT, NO ESCALATION
When volume spiked or something broke, the Exceeds hires redesigned the process and
contained the damage personally, so the customer or the team never felt it. They did not
solve problems by hiring, escalating, or presenting upward.

  Rohan    - 18 months of sharply higher documentation volume (air-freight expansion)
             handled "with no additional headcount... through process redesign". Legacy
             vendor migration under time pressure, no data loss.
  Sunita   - Weekend workflow rebuild "to ensure no disruption". Pharma account to 12
             countries with CDSCO/WHO-GMP requirements.
  Meghna   - Customs hold at 7pm, resolved overnight "before the client became aware".
             Covered a departed colleague's 8 accounts for 3 months, "without additional
             headcount - no churn". "No escalation to management in the last 14 months."
  Aditya   - Won 3 accounts from a competitor DURING a port congestion incident the
             competitor handled badly. "No account manager layer between the client and
             execution."
  Lavanya  - Wrote both the internal and customer-facing post-mortems after a 4-hour
             outage and owned follow-ups to closure.

  Comparison group: Rahul scaled by building a 4-person team. Vikram's CV is about
  managing stakeholders, quarterly roadmap presentations to the CTO and VP Sales, and
  process for a 4-person PM team. Preetham's incident work was an assigned on-call
  rotation inside a 12-engineer team. The Meets/Below CVs also contain zero failures,
  losses or crises - every line is a win or a % of target.

Why this is not in the JD: the JD says "comfort operating without structure". That is
about tolerating ambiguity. Pattern C is about what the person DOES when things break
under load: they redesign, contain, and protect the customer - with no one to hand it to.

Two supporting signals (used inside the criteria, not as separate patterns):
  - Ops-to-system translation. Rohan "translates field requirements into engineering spec
    without a product layer"; Sunita scoped and ran an FMS migration end to end; Lavanya's
    discovery found the carrier slot-booking mismatch and wrote the first API docs;
    Meghna turned an undocumented product limitation into a bug report and ran the 6-week
    fix with PM and engineering. They stand on the seam between the warehouse floor and
    the software.
  - Sole ownership. Rohan "most senior engineer in the room"; Sunita independent
    consultant; Lavanya "sole PM" whose calls engineering "trusted immediately. She
    doesn't hedge."; Aditya runs the full enterprise cycle alone. Vikram (4-person PM
    team) and Preetham (1 of 12 engineers) always had a layer beside or above them.


3. SCORING RULES (apply to every criterion, both roles)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Score each criterion 0-4 using the anchors given.
- Weighted score = sum over criteria of (score / 4) x weight. Range 0-100.
- EVIDENCE OR ZERO: every score above 0 must quote or closely paraphrase a specific line
  from the CV (a document type, a volume, a counterparty, a named artefact, an adoption
  number). Adjectives in a summary ("hands-on", "ownership mindset", "passionate about
  logistics") earn nothing on their own.
- Count what the person did, not what their team or company did.
- Where evidence is ambiguous, score the lower anchor and put the doubt into the
  "probe in interview" note - the interview is where ambiguity gets resolved, not the CV.
- Every candidate is scored against BOTH the PM and SPM rubric regardless of which role
  they applied for (a strong PM applicant may be a viable SPM, and vice versa).
- Suggested bands (weighted score):
      75-100  Strong shortlist - interview this week
      55-74   Shortlist - interview if the top band is thin
      35-54   Hold - only with a specific reason written down
      0-34    Do not advance (still receives a warm, human-reviewed rejection)
  Arjun decides. The score recommends; it never rejects on its own.

4. PM RUBRIC  (Product Manager - first PM on the core operations platform)
==========================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PM-1  OPERATOR'S CHAIR
Weight: 30%
Source pattern: A  |  Anchored on: Lavanya (Exceeds) vs Vikram (Meets) - same role
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
Has personally held a role where they were accountable for live freight, logistics,
supply-chain or port operations - not a role that sold to, analysed, or built software
for those operations. The CV names the operational objects they handled (Bills of Lading,
shipping bills, DOs, customs holds, carrier allocation, detention, exceptions, slot
bookings) and gives a volume or cadence (shipments per month, accounts per day). For a PM
this can be a pre-product career (Lavanya's 3 years at a 3PL is the model) or an
ops-heavy role inside a logistics tech company.

  4 - 2+ years in a frontline ops seat at a forwarder / CHA / 3PL / port / carrier, with
      named documents or processes AND volumes. (Rohan, Sunita, Lavanya, Meghna.)
  3 - 1-2 years in such a seat, OR a customer-facing commercial/CS role that sat inside
      daily operations (Aditya at the JNPT terminal).
  2 - Frontline ops in an adjacent operations-heavy domain (field service, manufacturing
      floor, last-mile, e-commerce fulfilment ops) with volumes and consequences.
  1 - Built or managed software FOR logistics users from the office; discovery via
      calls/interviews only. (Preetham's 3PL API integrations.)
  0 - No operational exposure. (Vikram, Rahul.)

Probe in interview: "Walk me through a morning when a shipment you were responsible for
got stuck. Who called you, what document was wrong, what did you do in the first hour?"
Red flag: talks about "the user" in the abstract; cannot name a document or counterparty.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PM-2  UNASKED BUILD, ORGANIC ADOPTION
Weight: 25%
Source pattern: B  |  Anchored on: Rohan, Sunita, Lavanya, Meghna
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
Can show at least one tool, process or feature they started without being assigned, that
fixed a problem they saw in someone's workflow, and that OTHER people adopted voluntarily
and kept using. The CV states who adopted it and how fast or how widely (e.g. "adopted by
the 12-member ops team within two weeks", "used by 2 other regional teams", "now the team
standard"). Scrappy is fine - Excel first, software later is the pattern (Lavanya's
dashboard, Rohan's tracker).

  4 - 2+ unasked builds with named adoption by people outside their own reporting line,
      still in use or productised. (Rohan, Lavanya.)
  3 - 1 clear unasked build with measured adoption beyond themselves. (Sunita, Meghna.)
  2 - Self-initiated improvement adopted within their own immediate team, or an artefact
      outside their remit with thin adoption evidence. (Aditya's case-study programme.)
  1 - Artefacts that are standard outputs of their own job (a PM writing a PRD template,
      a marketer running case studies, an engineer building a monitor for own service).
      (Vikram, Rahul, Preetham.)
  0 - Only assigned roadmap/deliverables listed.

Probe in interview: "What is something you built that nobody asked for? Who uses it today,
and how did they find out about it?"
Red flag: "shipped 12 features" with no line about who chose to use them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PM-3  ABSORBS THE BREAK
Weight: 20%
Source pattern: C  |  Anchored on: Meghna, Rohan, Sunita
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
The CV contains at least one moment where something broke or load spiked - a hold, an
outage, a vendor change, a departing colleague, a volume surge, a lost deal - and the
candidate contained it themselves through process redesign or direct action, WITHOUT
adding headcount or escalating, and the customer/team was protected. They describe the
failure honestly, not just the win.

  4 - 2+ concrete break-and-contain episodes with the outcome stated ("before the client
      became aware", "no additional headcount", "no disruption", "no churn"). (Meghna,
      Rohan, Sunita.)
  3 - 1 clear episode, or a candid loss/kill with a documented lesson that changed
      practice. (Lavanya's outage post-mortem and 2 killed features; Aditya's lost-deal
      post-mortem.)
  2 - Handles incidents inside an assigned process (on-call rota, escalation path), with
      results.
  1 - Mentions pressure or "fast-paced environments" with no specific episode.
      (Preetham at best.)
  0 - CV is 100% wins and % of target; problems are solved by hiring or presenting
      upward. (Vikram, Rahul.)

Probe in interview: "Tell me about the worst week in your last job. What broke, what did
you change, and who never found out it broke?"
Red flag: every answer ends with "so I escalated" or "so we hired".

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PM-4  OPS-TO-SPEC TRANSLATION
Weight: 15%
Source: supporting signal  |  Anchored on: Rohan, Lavanya, Meghna
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
Has converted something observed on the operations floor into a concrete change engineers
could build, and can name the specific workflow mismatch they found. Lavanya's discovery
uncovered how carriers actually confirmed slot bookings and the pivot cut that account's
tickets by 60%; Rohan translated field requirements into spec "without a product layer";
Meghna turned an undocumented limitation hurting 4 accounts into a bug report and ran the
fix with PM and engineering. Volume of interviews does not count; the specific mismatch
found does.

  4 - Names a specific ground-level workflow mismatch they found AND the shipped change AND
      an operational result (tickets, delays, exceptions, adoption).
  3 - Clear example of ops -> spec translation with a result, less specific on the
      mismatch.
  2 - Worked alongside engineering on ops-facing features, but insight came from others.
  1 - Discovery described as method/volume ("40+ user interviews", "JTBD framework") with
      no specific operational insight. (Vikram.)
  0 - No evidence of bridging ops and product/engineering.

Probe in interview: "What is one thing users told you that turned out to be wrong once you
watched them work?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PM-5  SOLE OWNER
Weight: 10%
Source: supporting signal  |  Anchored on: Lavanya (sole PM) vs Vikram (1 of 4 PMs)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
Has owned a product area, account book or process end to end where they were the only
person accountable - no senior PM, manager layer or committee making the call. The CV says
so explicitly ("sole PM", "no account manager layer", "independent", "most senior in the
room") and shows a decision they made and stood behind.

  4 - Sole owner of an area with a named consequential call they made (e.g. killing a
      feature customers asked for, based on usage data).
  3 - Sole owner, decisions implied but not named.
  2 - Owned a sub-area with a senior above who set direction.
  1 - One of several peers in a structured function; presents upward for decisions.
  0 - Execution role only.

Probe in interview: "What is a call you made that your manager would not have made? What
happened?"

PM WEIGHTS: 30 + 25 + 20 + 15 + 10 = 100%

Why the PM weights look like this:
- Operator's Chair (30) is the only pattern present in 5 of 5 Exceeds hires and 0 of 3
  others, and it is exactly what separated Lavanya from Vikram in the same role.
- Unasked Build (25) is the best predictor of the JD's own 6-month test: "features
  customers use without being asked to".
- Absorbs the Break (20) matters because the first PM will be alone when things break.
- Translation (15) and Sole Owner (10) are real but more coachable at the 2-4 year level,
  and the PM reports directly to Arjun, who can cover some decision-making early.

5. SPM RUBRIC  (Senior Product Manager - integration & data layer, most senior PM)
==========================================================================================
Same criterion names as PM so scores are comparable, but the bar is higher on every
anchor, and weight shifts toward independence and integration-seam work, because the SPM
owns decisions "we will feel two years from now" with no committee and no Head of Product.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SPM-1  OPERATOR'S CHAIR
Weight: 25%
Source pattern: A  |  Anchored on: Rohan, Sunita
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
Has held accountability for live logistics operations AND understands the systems those
operations run on: they have used or migrated the forwarder/customs stack themselves
(CargoWise, ICEGATE/customs EDI, TMS, port portals, carrier systems). Rohan (CHA ops ->
freight-tracking engineer) and Sunita (documentation -> ran an FMS migration) are the
models: they know what the integration has to survive because they lived the manual
version.

  4 - 2+ years in a frontline ops seat AND hands-on with the operational systems or
      data flows (FMS, customs EDI, TMS, carrier/port portals), with volumes.
  3 - 2+ years frontline ops, lighter systems exposure; OR long ops-embedded role at a
      logistics tech company working inside customer operations.
  2 - Ops-heavy adjacent domain (supply chain, field ops, fulfilment) with systems
      exposure.
  1 - Built integrations for logistics from the office only (Preetham).
  0 - No operational exposure.

Probe: "Draw me the path a Bill of Lading takes from your customer's system to the carrier
and back. Where does it break?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SPM-2  UNASKED BUILD, ORGANIC ADOPTION
Weight: 15%
Source pattern: B  |  Anchored on: Rohan (weekend prototype -> core platform feature)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
At SPM level the bar is not a spreadsheet the team used - it is an unasked build that
became part of the platform or the organisation's standard way of working. Rohan's BoL
verification module (weekend prototype -> 30 users in month 1 -> core feature) is the
model.

  4 - Self-initiated build that became a core product capability or a cross-team standard,
      with adoption numbers.
  3 - 2+ unasked builds adopted beyond own team, not yet productised.
  2 - 1 unasked build with adoption inside own team.
  1 - Only standard artefacts of own role.
  0 - Only assigned deliverables.

Probe: "Which thing you built without permission is now something the company depends on?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SPM-3  ABSORBS THE BREAK
Weight: 20%
Source pattern: C  |  Anchored on: Rohan (vendor migration), Sunita (vendor format change)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
Has contained a SYSTEM-level break - a data vendor failing, an upstream format changing
without notice, an outage, a migration under time pressure - and protected customers
while fixing the root cause. Both Rohan (legacy data-vendor migration, no data loss, 60%
less lag) and Sunita (FMS vendor changed its export format overnight; weekend redesign,
retained permanently) did exactly the kind of work the integration layer will throw at
this role. Writes the post-mortem and changes the standard afterwards.

  4 - 2+ system-level break-and-contain episodes with customer impact stated and a
      lasting fix/standard left behind.
  3 - 1 system-level episode, or several operational ones with a durable fix.
  2 - Operational firefighting only, no root-cause or standard change.
  1 - Generic pressure claims, no episode.
  0 - Wins-only CV; problems handed upward or solved by hiring.

Probe: "An upstream carrier API changes its schema on a Friday night. Tell me about the
last time something like that actually happened to you."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SPM-4  OPS-TO-SYSTEM TRANSLATION
Weight: 20%
Source: supporting signal  |  Anchored on: Rohan, Sunita, Lavanya
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
Has stood on the seam between operations and technical systems and made architectural
calls there: scoping migrations, choosing what to build vs configure vs leave alone,
documenting integrations so customers onboard faster. Evidence: Rohan translating field
requirements into engineering spec with no product layer and leading a vendor migration;
Sunita scoping a paper-to-cloud FMS migration, managing the vendor, training staff and
monitoring go-live; Lavanya writing the first API documentation (-2 weeks on integration
timelines).

  4 - Led at least one integration/migration end to end (scope, vendor or partner, rollout,
      post-go-live) AND names a build-vs-configure-vs-avoid decision with its outcome.
  3 - Led an integration/migration end to end, decision logic less explicit.
  2 - Contributed to integrations as one of several owners.
  1 - Integration work only from the engineering side, no ops context (Preetham).
  0 - No integration or ops-system work.

Probe: "Tell me about an integration you decided NOT to build. How did you explain that to
sales?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SPM-5  SOLE OWNER
Weight: 20%
Source: supporting signal  |  Anchored on: Sunita (independent), Rohan ("most senior in
the room"), Lavanya ("She doesn't hedge.")
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What a strong candidate looks like:
Has been the final decision-maker - not a contributor - for a product area, platform or
client operation for a sustained period, with no senior layer to escalate to, and can name
calls they made that had long-term consequences and that they lived with. For SPM this is
the most important difference from PM: the JD offers "no committee that approves product
decisions" and a path to Head of Product.

  4 - 2+ years as the most senior owner of an area (sole/lead PM, head of function,
      independent operator) with named long-horizon calls and their consequences,
      including at least one that went wrong.
  3 - Sole owner for 1-2 years, calls named.
  2 - Senior contributor with a strong manager above setting direction.
  1 - One of several peers; decisions made in reviews/committees (Vikram).
  0 - Execution role only.

Probe: "What decision are you still living with from two years ago? Would you make it
again?"

SPM WEIGHTS: 25 + 15 + 20 + 20 + 20 = 100%

Why the SPM weights differ from PM:
- Sole Owner rises 10 -> 20: the SPM is the most senior PM with no Head of Product.
- Ops-to-System Translation rises 15 -> 20: the SPM owns the integration and data layer,
  exactly the seam Rohan and Sunita worked on.
- Unasked Build drops 25 -> 15: at 5-8 years nearly every strong candidate has built
  things; the discriminator shifts to whether it became platform, captured in the higher
  anchor.
- Operator's Chair drops 30 -> 25 only because the anchor is harder; it is still the
  single largest criterion.

7. WHAT THIS RUBRIC DELIBERATELY IGNORES (score 0 weight, do not let it leak in)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Years of experience, job titles, seniority labels (already in the JD; Vikram had more PM
  years than Lavanya).
- PM certifications and communities: Product School, Reforge, CPO certificates. Both PMs
  held a Product School certificate; one exceeded, one did not. It is not a signal.
- College / MBA brand (XLRI, NIT, St. Stephen's appear across both groups).
- Company brand or size. Large, structured employers (3,200-person Cartexa) were, if
  anything, a negative context.
- Tool lists and frameworks (JIRA, Figma, JTBD, OKRs).
- Conference talks, "thought leadership".
- Revenue / ARR / pipeline numbers on their own. They count only when they come from an
  unasked build or a contained break.
- Name, gender, age, photo, location of origin, or any personal detail. These are
  stripped at extraction and must never reach the scoring step.
